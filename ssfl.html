01 · Overview

A lender for low-income households

Spandana Sphoorty Financial Limited (SSFL) is a Non-Banking Finance Company (NBFC). Its lending programme aims to strengthen the socio-economic status of low-income households, particularly women, in rural and urban areas, by providing financial services that improve livelihoods, establish identity and enhance self-esteem. Spandana follows both group-based and individual micro-credit lending models.

Its field teams run every loan through FIMO, an offline-capable Android app used by Loan Officers and Branch Managers to survey villages, enrol borrowers, run group training and collect repayments.

Project goal

Spandana Sphoorty's primary objective is to redesign the current LOS/LMS application and build a new customer self service portal, with an expansive focus beyond its existing features. A thorough research is to be conducted to comprehend user pain points, aspirations, and identify opportunities to enhance the experience and operational efficiency. The new portal is to be smartphone and tablet compatible.

02 · The problem

A loan app that worked against its users

Field Officers and Branch Managers relied on a loan management system that was fragmented, unintuitive and poorly aligned with their daily workflows. It caused frequent errors, slow processes and operational inefficiency, especially in rural areas with limited digital literacy. Growth targets and the need for faster disbursal made a redesign critical.

5–7 minto re-register an existing borrower

95–98%of users work in the field, often offline

Baseline figures shared by SSFL during the stakeholder workshop.

For SSFL

Onboard customers faster, sell more repeat and top-up loans, and give field staff the support they need without calling the branch or head office.

For users

Loan Officers and Branch Managers needed to finish enrolment, verification and collection quickly and accurately, often in villages with poor connectivity.

For design

Turn a 20+ module app built around back-office steps into a task-led tool that works on phones and tablets, online and offline.

What made it hard
  • A long, multi-stage loan process with many checks and hand-offs.
  • Users with different levels of digital literacy and languages.
  • Offline work had to keep working, with reliable sync.
  • Key data lived only in the desktop portal, not on mobile.
  • Two products in scope: the field app and a customer self service portal.
  • Research spread across 11 states with very different branches.

Discover: stakeholder workshop

We started with a workshop at SSFL's Hyderabad office with business, product, IT, technology and operations teams. Over three sessions we walked through the current app and business process, identified gaps and innovation opportunities, studied competitors, identified personas, and discussed the tech stack. We left with shared objectives and KPIs for the redesign.

Business objectives
  • Reduce customer onboarding time
  • Upsell top-up loans to eligible customers
  • Increase customers taking subsequent loans
  • Give Loan Officers support inside the app
KPIs agreed
  • Time to onboard a customer
  • Quality of information per page and time spent
  • Relationship extended beyond renewal

Understanding the loan process

A group loan passes through five stages, from creating a village to collecting repayments. Each stage involves several checks and hand-offs between the Loan Officer, Branch Manager and back office.

06 · Field research

Eleven branches in eleven states

We chose branch locations based on loan volume, geographic diversity (deep rural and town/rural) and operational complexity, and shortlisted participants with different experience levels and digital adoption. The team then visited each branch to:

  • Interview Branch Managers and Loan Officers about their goals, tasks and pain points, with a walkthrough of FIMO.
  • Observe in context: join them in the field to see centre meetings, house visits and collections, and how FIMO was really used.
  • Benchmark competitor apps and processes for JLG lending.
07 · What we learned

From raw data to actionable insights

In Miro, we organised every interview, observation and piece of app feedback into high-level goals, daily tasks, operational challenges, aspirations, app feedback and field insights, for each branch and each role. We then mapped task flows and overlaid pain points at each step to find the moments of friction.

We saw

Officers cropped and filtered Aadhaar and Voter ID photos by hand before upload, and typed Voter ID details twice.

What it meant

Data entry, not the process itself, was what made enrolment slow and error-prone.

What we did

Guided document capture with built-in filters, and OCR to fill details automatically.

We saw

The dashboard was a placeholder. Branch Managers passed on eligibility lists and product updates by hand.

What it meant

Officers had no single view of their day, their targets or their opportunities.

What we did

A task-led dashboard with today's work, newly eligible customers and incentive progress.

We saw

The demand table needed constant horizontal scrolling, and promise-to-pay dates were kept in a paper journal.

What it meant

Collection screens hid the information officers needed most during a centre meeting.

What we did

One card per borrower with dues, overdue amount, status and PTP tracking.

We saw

Long lists had no search. Finding a loan ID meant leaving the page and entering Aadhaar, centre and village.

What it meant

Officers think in village → centre → group → member. The app did not.

What we did

Global search across members, villages, centres and groups, with filters.

09 · Information architecture

From back-office steps to daily tasks

The old app put 20+ modules in one left menu, in the order the back office processes a loan. The new structure is built around what officers do each day.

10 · Key design decisions

How we made design decisions

Every decision traced back to a finding from the field. The 4 below show how we compared options and accepted trade-offs.

Decision · Dashboard

A dashboard built around today's tasks

Problem
The existing dashboard was an empty placeholder. Officers had no view of their day, targets or opportunities.
Options
  1. Concept 1: performance first, with task tiles and an eligible list
  2. Concept 2: a task list by group, with a branch leaderboard
Our choice
Task tiles from Concept 1, refined: colour-coded counts for Collection, Enrolment, CGT and LUC, then New eligible and Sampark calls, then incentive and performance.
Trade-off
The detailed task list and leaderboard moved one tap away, behind "View all".
Result
One screen answers "what do I do today?", "who can I sell to?" and "how am I doing?".
Decision · Enrolment

Capture documents once, correctly

Problem
Registering a new borrower took 10 minutes. Officers cropped and filtered ID photos manually and re-typed details.
Options
  1. Keep one long form, add OCR
  2. Break enrolment into steps, with guided capture and OCR
Our choice
A 6-step flow with progress shown. In-app capture with a frame guide, auto colour and filter options, and OCR-based Quick Fill.
Trade-off
More screens to move through, but each is shorter and clearly shows what is left.
Result
Removes manual cropping and duplicate typing, directly targeting the onboarding-time KPI.
Decision · Collections

Every borrower's status, in context

Problem
The demand table scrolled sideways, multiple loans showed as separate rows, PTP dates lived on paper, and only 1.12% of customers took top-up loans.
Options
  1. Improve the table
  2. One card per borrower, with status and actions
Our choice
Borrower cards with a colour stripe and tag (Overdue, NPA, Eligible for top-up), demand and OD fields, payment mode, and a single "Collect" total for the group.
Trade-off
Fewer borrowers fit on one screen than in a table.
Result
No sideways scrolling, PTP tracked in the app, and top-up eligibility visible during the centre meeting, when the officer can act on it.
Decision · Navigation and search

Find anyone, the way officers think

Problem
Long lists had no search, and finding a loan meant leaving the current task.
Options
  1. Add search to each list
  2. One global search, plus a bottom bar for daily needs
Our choice
Global search with filters for members, villages, centres and groups, and a bottom bar with Home, Lists, Sync and More. Sync is always one tap away for offline work.
Trade-off
Less-used modules sit one level deeper, under More.
Result
Officers reach any borrower from anywhere, and sync status is always visible.
